# Team Assignment 2.3 Module 2 Review - Team Mind Map

## Assignment Type

Team assignment - **10 points**

## Learning Objectives

By completing this activity, students should be able to:

* consolidate individual understanding into a shared representation;
* organize information hierarchically;
* show meaningful relationships between concepts; and
* explain computational thinking as a connected process.

## Duration

Recommended working time: **50-70 minutes**

## AI Policy

No AI assistance is allowed.

## Task Description

Share and compare the individual concept reviews. Create one team mind map with **Computational Thinking** as the central idea. You may use **Canva** or **Google Drawings**. Or you may even draw on your iPad. But make sure they are readable even on the projector screen.

The mind map must include:

* decomposition;
* abstraction;
* pattern recognition; and
* algorithmic thinking or algorithm design.

These are only the starting concepts. Your finished mind map must contain **at least 10 meaningful components** drawn from the Module 2 materials. Examples, sub-concepts, and clearly labelled relationships may count as components.

Do not create a flat list. Use branches, sub-branches, arrows, labels, or short connecting phrases to show how the concepts relate.

Every team member must be able to explain at least one part of the final mind map.

## Submission and File Naming Format

The coordinator submits a **viewable Canva or Google Drawings link** in the assignment's private comment. Include the team name and team members listed alphabetically by first name on the mind map. Check that the instructor can open the link before submitting it.

```text
ICCS102 M2 Review Team - [TeamName]
```

## Rubric - 10 Points

| Criterion | Complete (2 points) | Mostly complete (1.5 points) | Developing (1 point) | Missing or insufficient (0 points) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concept accuracy and coverage | Includes the four required starting concepts and at least 10 meaningful, accurate components from Module 2. | Meets the component requirement, but one or two ideas are unclear, repetitive, or inaccurate. | Includes fewer than 10 meaningful components or contains several misunderstandings. | Provides little accurate Module 2 content. |
| Relationships and hierarchy | Uses clear branches, sub-branches, arrows, or connecting phrases to show meaningful relationships and levels of importance. | Shows a sensible overall structure, but some relationships or levels are unclear. | Organizes concepts mainly as a list with few meaningful connections. | Shows no useful structure or relationships. |
| Explanation and examples | Uses concise labels, explanations, or examples that make the team's understanding visible. | Most components are understandable, but some need explanation or a stronger example. | Many components are only named and do not communicate what the team understands. | Provides almost no explanation beyond isolated terms. |
| Clarity and team identification | The mind map is readable, visually organized, and clearly displays the team name and members alphabetically by first name. | The mind map is generally readable and identified, with one minor clarity or identification problem. | The mind map is difficult to follow or has several missing identification details. | The work is unreadable or cannot be identified as the team's submission. |
| Correct submission | The coordinator submits a working viewable Canva or Google Drawings link in the private comment using the required submission title. | The correct link is submitted but has one minor access, title, or private-comment issue that is promptly corrected. | The link requires substantial correction, uses the wrong submission location, or cannot initially be accessed. | No usable link is submitted. |
